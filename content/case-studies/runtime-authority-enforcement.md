## Updated August 2026

This case study has been reissued and rebaselined to the current ControlPointAI systems-engineering and technical-governance framework.

The scenario examines a hypothetical naval nuclear-maintenance availability in which AI may assist with engineering analysis, work-package development, material review, execution support, testing, and readiness assessment. The central issue is not whether AI can perform those functions. It is whether valid human authority, technical accountability, configuration control, and evidence remain connected to consequential execution as conditions change.

### What the Updated Case Demonstrates

The reissued case expands the original runtime-authority concept to include:

- Data-flow and authority-flow mapping
- Execution-bound control points
- Runtime revalidation as conditions change
- Accountable human technical authority
- As-built configuration control
- As-executed evidence and reconstruction
- Traceability between the approved baseline and actual execution
- Intervention, escalation, and recovery of control

The scenario is deliberately hypothetical. It does not reproduce classified information, CUI, NOFORN material, proprietary information, controlled drawings, actual shipboard configurations, non-public acceptance criteria, real inspection results, or internal Navy maintenance records.
